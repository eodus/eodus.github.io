"ChatGPT said:\n\n- Skill atrophy through cognitive offloading: When AI routinely writes, codes, analyzes, and solves problems for us, we practice the underlying skills less. We may produce better-looking work while becoming less capable of producing it independently. The danger is losing not just execution skills, but the judgment needed to recognize mistakes.\n\n- Pathological confirmation loops: AI systems tend to accommodate users' framing, validate assumptions, and produce plausible justifications for existing beliefs. Repeated prompting can turn an initial hunch into an apparently well-supported conclusion, creating an illusion of independent corroboration where there is none.\n\n- The inversion of expertise: Competent people become reviewers of machine-generated output rather than practitioners exercising their expertise. But reviewing is not equivalent to creating: errors can be difficult to spot without reconstructing the reasoning, and reviewing mountains of plausible output can exhaust the attention needed for meaningful scrutiny.\n\n- Accountability without agency: AI makes generating output cheap, so organizations reward volume, speed, and apparent productivity. Humans are left approving, correcting, and taking responsibility for work they increasingly didn't originate and may not fully understand. The result is a dangerous mismatch: machines generate; humans rubber-stamp; nobody truly owns the thinking."
