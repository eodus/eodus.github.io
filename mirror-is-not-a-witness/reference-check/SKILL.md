# Reference check

## 1. Name the claim and standard

Record the exact target:

- proposition or wording and original language;
- claimed source, author, and work;
- edition, translator, date, or version when relevant;
- intended use and audience;
- what would count as sufficiently verified.

Choose the burden required by the task. An approximate memorable phrase, an attribution, an exact published quotation, and a signed court filing do not require the same procedure.

## 2. Build the source map

Let purpose, audience, jurisdiction, private or public use, language, medium, and required edition determine which sources matter. Prefer the shortest authoritative chain available:

1. original or official primary source;
2. publisher, court, standards body, or institutional record;
3. library catalogue, archive, or verified scan;
4. reputable secondary analysis;
5. search results only as leads.

For a translation, identify both the original text and the exact translator/edition. For a mathematical proof, legal citation, technical claim, or scholarly reference, identify the proposition, assumptions, authority, and source that actually establishes it.

## 3. Search and keep a ledger

For every useful lead, record:

- source identity, URL or catalogue identifier, page/section, date/version, and hash when useful;
- whether it is primary text, reported quotation, secondary analysis, search lead, negative search, conflict, duplicate, or unresolved gap;
- whether it was examined directly;
- what exact claim it supports or fails to support.

Preserve spelling variants, original-language wording, and contrary evidence. Do not turn `not found` into `does not exist` unless the search justifies that conclusion.

## 4. Return the evidence

Return:

- verified reference, quotation, or proposition, with enough surrounding context to preserve meaning;
- attribution, work, edition/translator/version, page or section, and stable source link;
- a list of sources actually examined;
- conflicts, negative searches, and remaining uncertainty;
- where to look next if the standard was not met.

If exact wording cannot be verified, say so and provide only a clearly labelled paraphrase or uncertain recollection. Never manufacture precision.

For legal, scholarly, or otherwise consequential use, inspect the authoritative source itself and verify that every quotation and citation supports the proposition for which it is used. The person who signs, publishes, or acts owns the final check.

Output: target and acceptance standard; source map; evidence ledger; verified result with context and attribution; uncertainty and next search.
